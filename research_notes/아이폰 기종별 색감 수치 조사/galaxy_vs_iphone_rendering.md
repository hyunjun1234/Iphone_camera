# Galaxy default rendering versus iPhone default rendering (colour and tone)

Research date: 2026-11-10. Scope: main rear camera, default settings, stills.

Evidence tags used on every finding:
- [M] measured on a chart or lab metric
- [L] lab tester observation in a controlled side-by-side (DXOMARK text and captions)
- [I] reviewer impression from sample photos
- [S] search-result snippet only, page not opened, unverified

## Key Question 1: What measured or carefully compared differences exist between Galaxy and iPhone photos of the same scene?

### Takeaway
The only chart-based saturation and white balance numbers found come from Bandicoot Lab (2025 to 2026 models): in daylight iPhones render more saturated than Galaxies (about 127% versus 115% of ColorChecker reference), and indoors at 4000K iPhones keep a much stronger yellow cast (9 to 13 points) while Samsung stays roughly steady. No Kelvin readings, Delta E pairs or histograms were found for any Galaxy and iPhone pair. DXOMARK gives lab sub-scores and same-scene captions for every generation.

### Cited Findings

Bandicoot Lab, 24-patch ColorChecker, main camera, default auto pipeline, published 2026-07-12 by Christian de Looper. Lighting: 1,000 lux at 5500K, 100 lux at 4000K, 10 lux at 3000K. Saturation is percent of chart reference. Blue-yellow bias is in "points", positive is yellow.
- [M] Daylight saturation, Samsung: brand average 115%, Galaxy range 112% to 123% (highest is Z Fold 7). Source: [Bandicoot report](https://bandicootlab.com/report/photo-saturation-brand)
- [M] Daylight saturation, Apple: brand average 121%. Five of six iPhones sit at 125% to 128%. iPhone 17e is 91%. Source: [Bandicoot report](https://bandicootlab.com/report/photo-saturation-brand)
- [M] Daylight blue-yellow bias: most brands lean yellow by about 2 to 5 points. "Apple sits at the warm end of that range", and "the iPhone's lean is comparable to a Galaxy's". Source: [Bandicoot report](https://bandicootlab.com/report/photo-saturation-brand)
- [M] Indoor (100 lux, 4000K): all six iPhones show 9 to 13 points of yellow bias, average 11.7. Next warmest brand is Honor at 4.4. Samsung: "desaturate moderately as light falls but keep white balance roughly steady" (no Samsung number given). Source: [Bandicoot report](https://bandicootlab.com/report/photo-saturation-brand)
- [M] Dark (10 lux, 3000K): Apple saturation falls from 127% in daylight to 90% on average, iPhone 17 Pro and Pro Max to 74%, yellow cast 8 to 12 points. Source: [Bandicoot report](https://bandicootlab.com/report/photo-saturation-brand)
- [M] Report conclusion: "Samsung oversaturates - not in this data." "iPhones lean warm - confirmed, but it's an indoor phenomenon, not a daylight one." Source: [Bandicoot report](https://bandicootlab.com/report/photo-saturation-brand)
- Limits of this source: the formula and unit of the bias "points" are not published, the per-model list is not readable (charts did not render), and the site names 62 devices from 2025 to 2026, so S22 to S25 and iPhone 13 to 16 are not confirmed to be in the data. Sources: [Bandicoot camera explainer](https://bandicootlab.com/explainer/how-we-test-cameras), [Bandicoot API page](https://bandicootlab.com/api)
- [L] Galaxy S26 Ultra main camera: saturation "relatively high" at 1,000 lux, closer to neutral at 100 lux, dynamic range "strong" in auto mode. Source: [Bandicoot S26 Ultra page](https://bandicootlab.com/phone/samsung-galaxy-s26-ultra)
- [L] iPhone 17 Pro main camera: saturation "pushed" in bright light, below target in low light, shift toward yellow-orange under warm mid light, dynamic range "moderate in auto mode, with heavy tone compression". Source: [Bandicoot iPhone 17 Pro page](https://bandicootlab.com/phone/apple-iphone-17-pro)

### Inferences
- Daylight chroma gap, 2025 to 2026 models: 127 / 115 = 1.10, so mainstream iPhones are about 10% more saturated than Galaxies on this chart. A Galaxy to iPhone daylight transform needs a small saturation increase, not a decrease.
- Indoor warm light is where the two brands diverge most: iPhone about 11.7 points yellow versus a brand that "keeps white balance roughly steady". A single global temperature offset cannot match both daylight and 4000K scenes.
- DXOMARK Artifacts and Noise scores point the same way in every generation: Samsung has more ringing (S9+ 62 versus iPhone X 82, S22 Ultra 66 versus iPhone 14 Pro 72) and smoother noise. The iPhone keeps finer grain.

### Gaps
- No white balance readings in Kelvin, no Delta E pairs, no histograms for any Galaxy and iPhone pair.
- Notebookcheck ColorChecker pairs not searched (stopped for context limit).
- Android Authority Imatest articles returned 403.
- Bandicoot per-model numbers and metric formula not obtained.

## Key Question 2: What are the consistently reported tendencies, by generation, and where do sources disagree?

### Takeaway
Stable across 2015 to 2025: Samsung exposes brighter, lifts shadows and highlights, has wider but flatter HDR, and shows more sharpening halos and noise smoothing. The iPhone has more contrast, darker shadows and finer grain. Saturation flipped: Samsung was called oversaturated up to the S23 generation, and from the S24 generation reviewers call the Galaxy flatter and the iPhone more vivid. White balance direction is disputed in every generation.

## Key Question 3: Does third-party camera API output on Galaxy differ from the stock Samsung camera?

### Takeaway
No controlled side-by-side of stock camera versus Camera2 or CameraX output on a Galaxy S was found. Documented facts: vendor processing reaches third-party apps only through Extensions (HDR, Night, Auto, Bokeh, Face Retouch), Scene Optimiser applies extra colour in the stock app, and the stock camera moved from sRGB to DCI-P3 with the S23.

### Cited Findings
- Extensions expose vendor-implemented Auto, Bokeh, Face Retouch, HDR and Night for preview and still capture only. HDR "takes several photos with various exposure values and merges them into one". Source: [Android Camera Extensions](https://developer.android.com/media/camera/camera-extensions)
- Google's list of devices with extensions includes Galaxy S10 series, S20 series, S21 5G (USA), S22 Ultra 5G (USA), S23, S23 FE, S23 Ultra, S24, S24+, S24 Ultra, S25, S25+, S25 Edge, S25 Ultra. The list is "not an exhaustive list". Source: [Android supported devices](https://developer.android.com/media/camera/supported-devices)
- On the Galaxy S22, third-party apps "have native access to all the extensions" and reach all three sensors through one logical camera. Basis: the CameraXInfo app and social posts, no Samsung statement. Source: [SamMobile 2022-07-29](https://www.sammobile.com/?p=162761)
- Galaxy S22: AI Autofocus, Night Mode, Portrait Video and Super HDR work inside Instagram, Snapchat and TikTok. Source: [SamMobile 2022-02-11](https://www.sammobile.com/news/galaxy-s22-excellent-camera-quality-instagram-snapchat-tiktok/)
- Galaxy S24: the stock camera saves Super HDR in Ultra HDR format. Samsung says Super HDR, HDR and multi-frame noise reduction "will also be available in third-party apps such as Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok, and WhatsApp". CameraX Ultra HDR output needs Android 14. Source: [SamMobile 2024-01-25](https://www.sammobile.com/news/android-phone-camera-ultra-hdr-third-party-apps/)
- Samsung: "Cameras prior to the Galaxy S23 used the sRGB color gamut", and the S23 supports DCI-P3 with D65 white point and 2.2 gamma after Samsung "resolved compatibility issues by collaborating with third party app companies". Posted 2023-04-18. Source: [Samsung CamCyclopedia](https://community.samsung.com/in/conversations/CamCyclopedia/Color-Displaying-a-wide-color-gamut/r2_13738723)
- Scene Optimiser: when a subject or scene is recognised "the optimised colour will be applied automatically", 20 scene modes, 30 on the S10. Source: [Samsung support](https://www.samsung.com/hk_en/support/mobile-devices/how-does-the-scene-optimiser-improve-my-photos/)
- CameraX default capture mode is `CAPTURE_MODE_MINIMIZE_LATENCY` ("optimize image capture for latency"). `CAPTURE_MODE_MAXIMIZE_QUALITY` optimises for quality. Source: [CameraX capture options](https://developer.android.com/media/camera/camerax/take-photo/options)
- [S] Samsung S24 ProVisual: AI applies detail, contrast and colour separately to faces, clothes, sky and trees. Source: [Samsung CamCyclopedia ProVisual](https://r2.community.samsung.com/t5/CamCyclopedia/ProVisual-Image-Quality-Experience/ba-p/16214921)
- [S] Galaxy S21+ owner: "only the RAW output or third-party apps are able to bypass all this weird colour processing". Source: [Samsung community thread](https://community.samsung.com/it/conversations/galaxy-s21-series/galaxy-s21-exynos-wrong-colours-on-main-camera-with-built-in/eu_4604055)
- [S] CameraX forum report: JPEG quality 95 in minimise-latency mode, 100 in maximise-quality mode. Source: [camerax-developers thread](https://groups.google.com/a/android.com/g/camerax-developers/c/PTeOQHxIK7c)

### Inferences
- Without extensions, the app most likely gets single-capture HAL output without Scene Optimiser and without the stock app's region-based tuning, so it should be less saturated per scene type and have less HDR than what reviews of the stock camera describe. Unverified by test.
- On S23 and later, stock photos can be P3-tagged while the app assumes sRGB. The colour space of CameraX JPEGs on these models is unverified and must be read from the ICC profile on a real device.
- Review findings in Key Questions 1 and 2 describe the stock camera. They are an upper bound on processing strength for the app's input, not a description of it.

### Gaps
- No stock versus CameraX comparison photos or measurements on any Galaxy S.
- Samsung developer forum threads could not be read (see Handoff).
- Whether Extensions HDR on Galaxy matches stock Auto HDR in tone is unknown.

## Key Question 4: What global adjustments do people apply to move a Galaxy photo toward an iPhone look?

### Takeaway
No new recipe with a before and after against a real iPhone photo of the same scene was found beyond the two already in docs/PUBLIC_COLOR_RECIPES.md. The only usable material is the direction implied by the measurements and same-scene reviews above.

### Cited Findings
- [S] Camera Assistant "Picture softening" has Off, Medium (50%) and High (100%). A user who found Samsung output oversharpened next to iPhone and Pixel reports that Medium looked more natural. Source: [Samsung community thread](https://r1.community.samsung.com/t5/photography/reduce-image-sharpening/m-p/22084324/highlight/true)
- A 2026-07 Korean Q&A asks how to make the S25 Ultra's warm look cooler like an iPhone. The answer gives no numbers and no iPhone comparison. Source: [Moyo Q&A](https://moyoplan.com/community/questions/ccf0a42d/갤럭시-s25-울트라-카메라를-아이폰처럼-바꾸고-싶어요-방법-있나요)
- Samsung's own change from S23 to S24 moved in the iPhone direction: less shadow lifting, darker darks, less HDR contrast. Source: [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/01/24/galaxy-s24-camera-processing-change/)

### Inferences
Direction for a Galaxy to iPhone global transform, derived from Key Questions 1 and 2, magnitudes unverified:
- Saturation: about +10% in daylight for S24 and later (127 / 115 from Bandicoot). Lower in low light, because iPhone saturation drops to 90% at 10 lux.
- Tone: more midtone contrast, darker shadows, less highlight lift. Stronger correction for S23 and earlier, which expose brighter.
- White balance: near zero offset in daylight. Warmer (yellow) under indoor warm light. This needs a scene-dependent term or separate daylight and indoor presets.
- Detail: reduce sharpening halos and add fine grain. Not possible in a colour LUT.

### Gaps
- No verified numeric recipe for any Galaxy and iPhone pair with real paired photos.
- Korean blog and YouTube recipes not reviewed (stopped for context limit).

## Key Question 5: Is there research or open paired data for mapping colour between phone cameras?

### Takeaway
Two datasets pair an iPhone with a Galaxy flagship: raw2raw (Galaxy S9 and iPhone X, raw only) and MetaISP (iPhone XR, Galaxy S22 Ultra, Pixel 6 Pro, phone-rendered RGB). Neither states a dataset licence. FODB has native JPEGs of 143 scenes from iPhone 6s, 7 and 8 Plus plus older and mid-range Galaxies, licence not stated. Global 3x3 and polynomial mappings performed poorly in the one paper that tested them between these two brands.

### Inferences
- MetaISP is the closest match to the app's problem (phone-rendered iPhone XR and Galaxy S22 Ultra images of the same scenes). It covers one Galaxy generation and an iPhone that is not in the app's list (XR sits between XS and 11).
- FODB is the only source found with native JPEGs from iPhone 6s, 7 and 8 Plus next to Samsung phones. Its Galaxies are not S-series flagships of the same years, so it fits per-iPhone target statistics better than Galaxy to iPhone fitting.
- SIDD sRGB files are not native phone renderings (gamma only, no tone mapping), so SIDD cannot be used to fit rendered looks.
- A global matrix or polynomial left Delta E near 10 to 19 between these two brands in raw space. A 3D LUT fitted from paired rendered images will likely also leave scene-dependent error, because both vendors apply local tone mapping.
- None of the datasets with an iPhone and Galaxy flagship pair is confirmed openly licensed. Use requires asking the authors.
