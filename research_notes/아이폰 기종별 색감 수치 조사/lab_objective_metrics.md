# Lab objective colour and tone metrics for nine iPhone cameras

Research date: 2026-11-10. Scope: default camera app, main rear camera, stills.
Labels: [M] measured number published. [S] published score, valid only inside one test protocol. [D] described by the lab or reviewer, no number. "Unverified" (미확인) means the page was not opened in this session.
Stopped early on a context limit. See Handoff at the end.

## 1. What objective colour data did DXOMARK publish for each model?

### Takeaway
The live DXOMARK pages give no Delta E, Kelvin, mired or saturation percent for any of the nine models. They give protocol-specific Exposure and Color sub-scores from the iPhone 8 onward, three measured exposure values (X, 8 Plus, 11), and written cast, saturation and contrast findings tied to lighting.

### Cited Findings
- 6s (2015-10-13). [S] Overall 82, Photo 82 in text (score panel shows 83), Video 80. No sub-scores on the page. [D] "slight yellow cast visible in some outdoor scenes"; compared with the iPhone 6 "the cast was slightly stronger and more noticeable on the 6s"; flash at 20 lux mixed with tungsten gave a "slightly pink tint"; "slight but consistent underexposure in extremely low light levels (5 lux)"; exposure differed between consecutive bright outdoor shots "due to the inconsistent activation of the HDR mode"; video: "Color shading is noticeable at times, particularly in low light conditions". ([DXOMARK 6s](https://www.dxomark.com/apple-iphone-6s-review-solid-and-compelling-performance/))

## 2. Did Imatest-based reviewers publish saturation, Delta C or Delta E, or white balance error?

### Takeaway
No Imatest-style colour number was found for any of the nine models from Imaging Resource, Tom's Guide, PCMag, DPReview, CNET, Tom's Hardware, AnandTech, Reviewed or Android Authority. The only chart-based saturation and warmth numbers found are from Bandicoot Lab for 2025 to 2026 iPhones, which are not target models.

### Cited Findings
- [M] Bandicoot Lab (2026-07-12), 24-patch ColorChecker, default auto pipeline, three conditions: 1,000 lux 5500K, 100 lux 4000K, 10 lux 3000K. Apple daylight saturation: six-iPhone average 121%, five iPhones at 125 to 128%, iPhone 17e at 91%. The article also states Apple falls from 127% in daylight to 90% in the dark. iPhone 17 Pro and Pro Max in the dark: 74%. Yellow bias at 100 lux 4000K: 9 to 13 for all six iPhones, average 11.7, where about 2 reads as neutral and past 4 to 5 "whites and skin tones visibly shift". Units of the bias are not defined. Models named: iPhone 17 family and 17e. ([report](https://bandicootlab.com/report/photo-saturation-brand), [method](https://bandicootlab.com/explainer/how-we-test-cameras))
- [M, informal] Birchtree (Matt Birchler, 2020-04-20), sky photos averaged over three shots, HSL: iPhone 11 Pro hue 218, same as Pixel 4; saturation 20% lower than Pixel 4; lightness 17% darker; telephoto 9% more saturated and 10% lighter than the wide lens. No chart, no Kelvin. ([Birchtree](https://birchtree.me/blog/iphone-11-pro-and-pixel-4-color-tests/))
- [D] Helbling, TE42 chart with iQ-Analyzer-X, iPhone 15 Pro: red and green tones deviate more than on the reference camera module; grayscale accuracy is very good; 24 MP images are software-sharpened with overshoot near 450 lp/ph; MTF50 1,350 lp/ph centre at 24 MP. Delta E values exist only in a figure. Capture app and format not stated. ([Helbling](https://helbling.ch/en/insights/how-the-image-quality-of-a-low-cost-camera-can-keep-up-with-an-iphone))
- [D] Labo Fnac, iPhone 11 Pro Max: no camera Delta E; rendering described as "très naturel" against competitors that boost colours; detail score 6.4 at 500 lux. ([Labo Fnac](https://leclaireur.fnac.com/test/29330-test-labo-de-lapple-iphone-11-pro-max-la-fin-des-compromis/))
- Android Authority's 2017 camera test measured Delta C 00, saturation and white balance from a ColorChecker, but for Android phones. Unverified, search summary only. ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/best-of-android-2017-camera-819624))

### Inferences
- If the 16 follows the 2025 to 2026 iPhones, daylight chroma is about 25% above reference and falls below 100% in dim warm light. This is an extrapolation across model years.

### Gaps
- Four targeted searches returned no iPhone page from Imaging Resource, Reviewed or Android Authority. Absence in search results is not proof that none exists.
- VCX-Forum score pages returned 404. TechRadar's 6s lab page returned no text.

## 3. Peer-reviewed or technical papers on these exact models

### Takeaway
No paper was found that reports ColorChecker Delta E, saturation or white balance bias for the default camera of any target model. The usable measured item is the JPEG tone response of the iPhone SE (2016), a 12 MP f/2.2 camera like the 6s. Dental papers give Delta E after external light or gray-card correction, not default rendering.

### Cited Findings
- [M] Burggraaff et al. 2019, Optics Express. Devices include iPhone SE, 6S and 7 Plus. iPhone SE JPEG response is nonlinear (single-pixel example r = 0.956, RAW r = 0.999). With sRGB-like fits at gamma 2.2 and 2.4, 99.9% of iPhone SE pixels had RMS relative differences above 7% and 10%. Best-fit gamma varied 1.7 to 2.6 across pixels, filters and devices, with deviations above 30% from sRGB profiles. "the dynamic range of the JPEG data is much smaller than that of the RAW data". JPEGs were taken with fixed white balance. Flat-field correction factors reached 2.42 (iPhone SE) and 2.03 (iPhone 6S). ([arXiv 1906.04155](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.04155), PDF text read)
- [M] Jorquera et al., J Prosthet Dent 128(4):784-792. iPhone XS with Smile Lite MDP and gray card white balancing: Delta E 2.34, DSLR 2.75, visual 5.32, threshold 3.7, 45 patients. ([record](https://investigadores.uandes.cl/es/publications/a-comparison-of-ceramic-crown-color-difference-between-different--2/))
- [M] JDMS study, iPhone X and Galaxy S9 plus, 45 participants: Kappa above 0.9, Delta E*Lab above 3.7 against a spectrophotometer; "The brightness of the image (L) significantly affects the accuracy of the color comparison." Per-phone values are not in the abstract. ([JDMS](https://he02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/JDMS/article/view/250569))
- [M] Durmaz Yilmaz et al., Int J Comput Dent 2/2025: iPhone cameras Delta E 2.68 to 2.90 against a spectrophotometer, custom software. iPhone models not in the abstract. ([Quintessence](https://www.quintessence-publishing.com/deu/en/article/5290621/international-journal-of-computerized-dentistry/2025/02/evaluierung-verschiedener-messmethoden-fuer-die-zahnfarbbestimmung))
- [M] Cebrián et al. 2022, Biosensors 12(5):341. iPhone SE and iPhone 8 JPEGs. iPhone 8 peroxide readings without colour correction were off by 25 to 86%, and by 4 to 29% with correction. Per-iPhone Delta E is in Supplementary Table S6, not read. ([PMC9139083](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9139083))
- [M] Kang 2026, arXiv: an unnamed smartphone against a DSLR on skin, Delta E00 mean 6.99, L* 77.56 vs 81.35, b* 8.96 vs 17.59. Not iPhone-specific. ([arXiv 2512.21988](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.21988v2))
- Gaiani et al. 2019 used iPhone X RAW through a custom app; the abstract has no numbers. ([ISPRS](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLII-2-W11/539/2019/))
- Turkish thesis on iPhone 11 and 13: no significant difference between them, Delta E under 3.7. Unverified, page returned 403. ([Tezara](https://tezara.org/theses/1013782))

### Inferences
- A plain sRGB or gamma 2.2 curve is a poor model of the 6s-era JPEG tone response. Gamma 2.2 fits better than 2.4, with residuals of at least 7%.

### Gaps
- Tominaga et al. 2021 (spectral sensitivities of phone cameras): the iPhone list was not confirmed.
- No dermatology, wound, pathology or agriculture paper with per-model default-camera bias was found in three searches.

## 4. Measured trend across generations

### Takeaway
No source measured one metric across these generations. The trend below comes from DXOMARK wording per model plus one exposure comparison of X and XS. It supports: persistent slight yellow lean, strongest warmth on the 8 under tungsten and on the 11, flattest tone on the XS, higher contrast with bright highlights from the 11 to the 15 Pro, and a neutral 16.

### Cited Findings
- 6s to 7: yellow cast outdoors on the 6s, light green cast in specific outdoor conditions and yellow under tungsten on the 7; the 7 writes DCI-P3 JPEGs. [D] ([6s](https://www.dxomark.com/apple-iphone-6s-review-solid-and-compelling-performance/), [7](https://www.dxomark.com/apple-iphone-7-camera-review-better-than-ever/))
- 7 to 8 and X: HDR handling rises ("One of the best HDR performances"); the 8 gains a tungsten and low-light cast; the X is neutral under tungsten and fluorescent, with strong reds. [D] ([8](https://www.dxomark.com/apple-iphone-8-reviewed-a-solid-performance-upgrade-over-the-iphone-7/), [X](https://www.dxomark.com/apple-iphone-x-top-performer-stills/))
- X to XS: [M] same scene, "iPhone X exposed for 1/60th of a second at ISO 40 whereas the iPhone XS exposed for 1/120th of a second at ISO 80." [D] "The iPhone XS merges exposures and reduces the brightness of the bright areas and reduces the darkness of the shadows."; "noise reduction removes a bit of detail and local contrast." ([Lux, 2018-10-01](https://www.lux.camera/iphone-xs-why-its-a-whole-new-camera/)). DXOMARK: yellow cast "a little stronger" than the X and wider dynamic range in shadows and highlights. ([XS Max](https://www.dxomark.com/apple-iphone-xs-max-review-flagship-imaging-power/))
- XS to 11: "high levels of contrast", "warm white balance and high saturation", clipped highlights; fewer casts than the XS Max. [D] ([11](https://www.dxomark.com/apple-iphone-11-camera-review/), [11 Pro Max](https://www.dxomark.com/apple-iphone-11-pro-max-camera-review/))
- 11 to 13: vivid with "a slightly warm touch", highlights "almost "aggressively" bright", limited dynamic range, skin-adaptive colour. Evidence is from the 13 Pro. [D] ([13 Pro retest](https://www.dxomark.com/apple-iphone-13-pro-camera-test-retested/))
- 13 to 15 Pro: Smart HDR "boosting color, contrast and brightness"; white balance largely neutral; highlight clipping reduced against the 14 Pro. [D] ([15 Pro](https://www.dxomark.com/apple-iphone-15-pro-camera-test/), [15 Pro Max](https://www.dxomark.com/apple-iphone-15-pro-max-camera-test/))
- 15 to 16: white balance moves from warm to neutral in the same scene; ultra-wide colour from "Saturated" to "Neutral". [D] ([16](https://www.dxomark.com/apple-iphone-16-camera-test/))
- Published sub-scores, Photo Exposure / Color: 8 89/77, X 90/79, XS Max 93/84, 11 Pro Max 96/87 (2017 and 2019 protocols); 11 104/115, 13 109/116 (later protocols); 15 Pro 123/124, 16 124/129 (v5). [S] (pages cited in section 1)

### Inferences
- Warmth order, most to least: 8 and 11, then XS, 6s, 13, then 7, X, 15 Pro, then 16.
- Global contrast order, flattest first: XS, then 6s and 7, then 8 and X, then 11, 13, 15 Pro, 16. Shadow lift is strongest on the XS. Highlight brightness is strongest on the 13 and 15 Pro.
- Saturation: 11 highest in bright light; X and 15 Pro vivid; XS and 16 closer to neutral; 6s to XS all lose saturation in low light.

### Gaps
- Review comparisons that state XS flatness against the 11 (MacSurfer, AnandTech) were seen only in search summaries. Unverified.
- No measured data on saturation of the 7's P3 output against the 6s.
